Thermodynamics & Engineering Principles of Refrigeration

Refrigeration is the branch of mechanical engineering and applied thermodynamics concerned with lowering and maintaining the temperature of an enclosed space below that of its surrounding environment by extracting heat and rejecting it elsewhere.

The Closed Hermetic Circuit: Four Primary Thermodynamic Transformations

In a single-stage vapour-compression system, a chemical refrigerant changes state continually between liquid and gas in a closed thermodynamic loop:

  1. Compression (Isentropic / Polytropic): The compressor draws in low-pressure, low-temperature superheated vapour from the suction line and compresses it mechanically, expending electrical work ($W_{in}$) to raise its pressure and saturation temperature above that of the available cooling medium.
  2. Heat Rejection & Condensation (Isobaric): In the condenser coil, hot discharge gas rejects sensible heat (de-superheating) and latent heat of vaporisation to ambient air or cooling water, condensing into a subcooled high-pressure liquid ($Q_{out}$).
  3. Throttling & Expansion (Isenthalpic): The high-pressure liquid passes through a restriction device (TXV, EEV, or capillary tube). The sudden pressure drop forces a fraction of the liquid to flash into vapour, instantly dropping its temperature to the evaporating saturation point.
  4. Heat Absorption & Evaporation (Isobaric): Inside the evaporator heat exchanger, the low-temperature refrigerant boils at low pressure, absorbing latent heat ($Q_{in}$) from the refrigerated cabinet or process fluid before returning to the compressor.

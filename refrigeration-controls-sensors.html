Refrigeration Controls and Sensors: Pressure Switches, Transducers and Automation

A refrigeration circuit cannot operate safely without an integrated network of electromechanical and electronic safety controls. Sensors continually monitor pressures, temperatures, and fluid levels to maintain operational stability and protect expensive compressors against loss of charge, extreme head pressures, and oil starvation.

Electromechanical Pressure Switches (Dual Pressure Controls)

The dual pressure switch (such as Danfoss KP15 or Ranco 012) combines both high-side and low-side bellows into a single robust housing wired in series with the compressor contactor coil:

Control Section Primary Function Typical Settings (R134a) Reset Mechanism
High Pressure (HP) Cutout Protects against condenser fan failure, air obstruction, or overcharge Cut-out: 1600 kPa (230 psig)
Differential: Fixed 300 kPa		 Manual Reset (forces technician investigation after trip)
Low Pressure (LP) Cutout Protects against loss of refrigerant, iced evaporator, or acts as thermostat Cut-out: 50 kPa (7 psig)
Cut-in: 200 kPa (29 psig)		 Auto Reset (initiates automated pump-down cycling)

The Automated Pump-Down Cycle

In commercial coolrooms and refrigeration systems, compressors are rarely cycled directly by a wall thermostat. Instead, an automated pump-down control sequence is employed to protect against off-cycle refrigerant migration:

  1. Thermostat satisfied: The temperature controller de-energises a normally-closed Liquid Line Solenoid Valve (LLSV) positioned immediately before the TXV.
  2. Refrigerant evacuates: The compressor continues running, pumping all liquid out of the evaporator and storing it safely in the condenser and liquid receiver.
  3. LP cutout activates: Once suction pressure drops below the low-pressure set point (e.g. 50 kPa), the LP switch opens its contact, shutting down the compressor.
  4. Off-cycle safety: During the off cycle, the low side remains dry, preventing liquid refrigerant from migrating into the cold compressor crankcase and condensing into the oil.
  5. Call for cooling: When the thermostat calls again, it re-opens the solenoid valve; refrigerant pressure surges into the low side, closing the LP switch and restarting the compressor without hard electrical inrush stress.

Oil Differential Pressure Controls

In large semi-hermetic reciprocating and screw compressors equipped with positive-displacement oil pumps, lubrication failure can destroy crank bearings within 60 seconds.

Net Oil Pressure Equation: The mechanical switch measures the differential between oil pump discharge pressure and suction crankcase pressure:
Net Oil Pressure = P_oil_pump - P_crankcase
If net oil pressure drops below 60 kPa (9 psi), an internal thermal bimetal heater begins counting down. If pressure is not restored within 90 to 120 seconds, the control trips out on mechanical lockout, stopping the compressor before catastrophic seizure occurs.

Electronic Sensors: NTC Thermistors & Ratiometric Transducers

Modern microprocessor controllers (Carel, Dixell, Eliwell) replace mechanical bellows with electronic solid-state instrumentation:

  • Negative Temperature Coefficient (NTC) Thermistors: Resistive sensors (typically 10kΩ at 25°C) providing ±0.2°C accuracy for cabinet air, defrost termination, and evaporator fin monitoring.
  • Ratiometric Pressure Transducers: Piezo-resistive ceramic sensors powered by 5V DC, outputting a linear 0.5V to 4.5V analogue signal proportional to gauge pressure. Microprocessors sample this voltage every 100 milliseconds to calculate saturation temperature and drive electronic expansion valves (EEVs).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a high-pressure cutout require a manual reset button?

An extreme high-pressure event indicates a dangerous condition—such as a seized condenser fan motor, water cooling failure, or severe overcharge. If the switch reset automatically, the compressor would continuously cycle on and off under extreme electrical stress until its internal motor windings melted.

What is the purpose of an electric crankcase heater?

Crankcase heaters keep the compressor oil sump 10°C to 15°C warmer than the coldest part of the refrigeration system during the off cycle. Refrigerant vapour naturally migrates and condenses into the coldest zone; keeping the oil warm prevents refrigerant from dissolving into the oil and causing destructive oil foaming on startup.
